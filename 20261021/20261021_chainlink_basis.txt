PRE-REGISTRATION (written before any Chainlink-vs-outcome number was computed)

Question
  15m BTC markets settle on Chainlink's 60s BTC/USD TWAP, not Binance spot. Near the
  close, does the Chainlink price's distance from its own window-open value predict the
  official outcome better than the market's own price already does?

Data
  var/poly15m.db, server copy of Oct 20 2026. Chainlink ticks (source chainlink_twap60,
  1 per second) exist from Sep 29 01:23 to Oct 15 15:02 UTC.
  Windows: opening >= Sep 29 01:30 UTC and closing <= Oct 15 15:00 UTC, official
  resolution 'up'/'down'.
  train = windows opening Sep 29 01:30 - Oct 8 23:59 UTC
  test  = windows opening Oct 9 00:00 UTC - Oct 15 (close <= 15:00)
  Oct 1-3 have no order books (CLOB feed hung), so those windows drop out of train.

Price definitions (CL = Chainlink TWAP, BN = Binance trade price)
  X(t) = latest tick of X at or before t.
  CL_open = CL(window_open_ts), CL_close = CL(window_close_ts); same for BN.
  Chainlink proxy outcome = up if CL_close >= CL_open.

Step 0: data validity gate (train windows only)
  Agreement of the Chainlink proxy with the official outcome must be >= 97%.
  Binance proxy agreement is reported alongside. If the gate fails, the timestamps or
  the feed don't match what settlement uses: stop and investigate, don't run Step 1-2.
  A tick is only used if it is no more than 5 s older than the time it stands for.

Checkpoints and samples
  Seconds before close: 180, 120, 90, 60, 30, 15.
  Groups: "mid" = {180, 120, 90}, "late" = {60, 30, 15}.
  A checkpoint is kept if CL and BN ticks are <= 5 s old and both tokens have a book
  snapshot <= 10 s old with a best bid and best ask.
  Market price: mid_up = (best_bid_up + best_ask_up) / 2.

Features
  sigma  = std dev of 1 s Binance price changes over the 300 s before the checkpoint
           (BN sampled every second by last value), in $ per sqrt(s), floored at 1.0.
           The same sigma is used for both feeds so the only difference between them is
           the price itself.
  z_cl   = (CL(t) - CL_open) / (sigma * sqrt(t_rem))
  z_bn   = (BN(t) - BN_open) / (sigma * sqrt(t_rem))
  Both clipped to [-6, 6].

Step 1: information test (same method as Sep 29)
  Logistic models fitted on train, separately per group, scored on test by log loss:
    M0  y ~ logit(mid_up)
    M1  y ~ logit(mid_up) + z_cl            <- the hypothesis
    M2  y ~ logit(mid_up) + z_bn            (reference only: is it Chainlink specifically,
                                             or any spot distance-to-strike?)
  Uncertainty: window-level bootstrap, 1,000 resamples, seed 20261021.
  Two groups are tested, so each uses a 97.5% CI (Bonferroni).
  INFORMATION PASS for a group: on test, mean(M1 - M0 log loss) < 0 with the whole
  97.5% CI below 0, AND the z_cl coefficient fitted on train is positive.

Step 2: trading rule (only meaningful if Step 1 passes; run and reported either way)
  For each group that passed Step 1, use M1's train fit to get q = P(up).
  At each kept checkpoint in that group, in time order, buy 1 share of the side with the
  larger q - ask - fee, if that exceeds margin m. At most one trade per window.
  Fee = 0.07 * p * (1 - p) per share (taker, src/poly15m/sim/fees.py).
  Fills at the best ask, book no older than 5 s. Graded by official settlement.
  m is chosen on train from {0.02, 0.04, 0.06, 0.08} by highest train PnL.
  TRADE PASS: test PnL > 0 and t >= 2. PROMISING: test PnL > 0 and t >= 1. Else FAIL.
  M0 with the same rule is reported for reference only.

Decision (fixed now)
  Info PASS and trade PASS  -> build a Chainlink-anchored model and pre-register a
                               forward paper run with a stop rule.
  Info PASS, trade not PASS -> the basis carries information that taker fees eat; the
                               only route left is maker orders, which paper can't test
                               (see Oct 21 notes). Not a reason to keep paper trading.
  Info FAIL                 -> stop strategy research on these markets.
  Gate FAIL                 -> fix the data first; no conclusion either way.

Known limits, stated up front
  - Not fully blind. Before writing this I had seen: Binance disagreed with settlement in
    103 of the 1,590 windows (6.5%), and traded windows where the feeds disagreed lost
    $228 in the Sep 28 - Oct 15 paper run. Both use outcomes. I have not computed any
    Chainlink-vs-outcome number, and the features, checkpoints, split and pass bars above
    were fixed before running anything.
  - Small sample: about 600 test windows, and the basis only matters when BTC is near
    the open late in the window. Only a large effect will pass. A FAIL here means "no
    evidence of a large effect", not proof of none.
  - Book data: the price_change fix is live, so books are continuous, but only the
    best bid/ask is used here.
  - The market's participants can watch Chainlink too; a FAIL is the expected outcome if
    they do.

Script: scripts/chainlink_basis.py (build, then analyze; results are appended below).

-----------------------INTERPRETATION 21 Oct 2026
Verdict under the pre-registered rule: Gate PASS, INFORMATION FAIL in both groups -> stop strategy
research on these markets.

Step 0. The recorded feed is the settlement price.
  - Chainlink proxy matched the official outcome in 951/951 train windows (100%); Binance in 893/951
    (93.9%). Timestamps and feed are right, and settlement really is "Chainlink TWAP at close >= at open".

Step 1. The market already prices Chainlink.
  - Adding z_cl to the market price changes test log loss by -0.0000 (mid) and +0.0002 (late); both
    97.5% CIs straddle 0 and are narrow (about +/-0.001). The fitted z_cl weights are ~0
    (-0.04, +0.04). This isn't a noisy null: the effect is pinned close to zero.
  - The market price on its own is well calibrated here (b_mid ~1.05-1.09; raw mid log loss is within
    0.001 of the fitted M0).
  - Binance distance-to-strike (M2) is no better and gets a negative weight, the same result as Sep 29.

Step 2. Nothing to trade.
  - At a 2c margin over fees, M1 found 82 trades in 578 train windows (-$3.44) and 1 test trade. With
    taker fees of ~1.75c per share at 50c, the market is never far enough from M1's probability to
    pay for crossing the spread.

Clarification (no deviation from the plan): 6 checkpoints x 1,585 windows = 9,510; 3,612 were kept.
  Most skips are one-sided books, where the winning token is bid at 0.99+ with no asks left (the
  market has already decided the window), and the Oct 1-3 windows with no book data. Both are
  dropped by the "best bid and best ask on both tokens" rule written before the run.

What this means
  - Every candidate has now failed: the Binance-momentum model (five ways, Sep 23 - Oct 15) and the
    settlement basis (today). The Binance-vs-settlement disagreements that cost the paper bot money
    were losses to a market that was already watching Chainlink, not an inefficiency left for us.
  - Per the decision rule: stop strategy research on these markets. Don't go live.
  - Optional, as a separate project only: maker (limit-order) strategies avoid the taker fee and earn
    rebates, but paper trading can't simulate queue position, so they could only be tested with a
    small amount of real money. Nothing in this test suggests an informational edge to carry there.
  - The recorder can be stopped, or kept only if that maker project is planned.
