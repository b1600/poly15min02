Data: var/calib/dataset.csv (scripts/market_calibration.py build)
  Each window with an official outcome, sampled at 840/720/600/480/360/240/180/120/60/30 s left.
  Model state (spot, sigma, deviation, p_model) comes from the step 5 base replay (var/step5/base.db),
  which uses the corrected open prices. Best bid/ask per token come from the recorded book_snapshots.
  The book snapshot must be <= 30 s old. Rows missing a bid or ask on either token are dropped.
  mid_up = (up_bid + up_ask) / 2. Probabilities are clipped to [0.01, 0.99] before taking logits.
  train = windows opening Aug 3 - Sep 22 (17,864 usable rows, 2,139 windows)
  test  = windows opening Sep 23 onward
  Groups by time left: early 840/720/600, mid 480/360/240, late 180/120/60/30.
  Uncertainty: window-level bootstrap (200 resamples) because rows in one window are correlated.

A. Market calibration (descriptive, shown for both periods)
   Per group: realized Up rate vs mid_up in 10 bins, and a logistic fit y ~ a + b*logit(mid_up).
   a = 0 and b = 1 mean calibrated. b > 1 means the market is too timid (longshots overpriced
   relative to favourites is b > 1 around the middle); b < 1 means it is overconfident.

B. Does the model add information? (fit on train, scored on test)
   Per group, fitted on train:
     M0: y ~ a + b*logit(mid_up)                      (market, recalibrated)
     M1: y ~ a + b*logit(mid_up) + c*logit(p_model)   (market + model)
   Test log loss for: raw mid_up, raw p_model, M0, M1.
   "Model adds information" only if the test log-loss difference M1 - M0 has a 95% bootstrap CI
   entirely below 0.

C. Is any of it tradeable?
   Per group, use M1 if its 5-fold window-grouped cross-validated log loss on train beats M0,
   otherwise M0. q = predicted P(Up).
   For each window, walk the checkpoints in time order, using only rows whose book is <= 5 s old.
   At the first checkpoint where either
       q     - up_ask   - fee(up_ask)   > m     (buy Up), or
       1 - q - down_ask - fee(down_ask) > m     (buy Down)
   buy 1 share of the side with the larger edge at its ask. One entry per window.
   PnL = (1 if that side won) - ask - fee, with fee = 0.07 * p * (1 - p) per share.
   m is chosen from {0.02, 0.04, 0.06, 0.08} by train PnL (in-sample, so the train number is optimistic).
   Test verdict for the chosen model and m:
     PASS         test PnL > 0 and per-trade t >= 2
     PROMISING    test PnL > 0 and 1 <= t < 2   (worth a forward paper run, not live money)
     FAIL         otherwise
   Also reported, for information only: the same rule using M0 alone (market bias, no model).

Known limits, stated up front
  - Top-of-book, 1 share: no depth, no queue, no latency. Quotes can be up to 5 s stale in C.
  - Books come from snapshot-only recording (the price_change bug), so quotes are coarser than live.
  - Sep 23 - 28 has been looked at before (steps 4 and 5), just not in this form. The test is not
    fully unseen. A PASS here justifies a forward paper run, nothing more.

-----------------------INTERPRETATION 29 Sep 2026
Verdict: FAIL on all three questions. There is no stable market miscalibration to trade, and the
fair-value model carries no information that the market price doesn't already have.

A. The market is close to calibrated, and its biases don't persist.
   - Early (8-14 min left): calibrated in both periods (a ~ 0, b ~ 1, CIs cover it).
   - Late (<= 3 min left), train: Up was overpriced. a = -0.23 (CI excludes 0); Up at 0.4-0.9 won
     5-11 points less often than priced.
   - Late, test: the sign flipped. a = +0.16, b = 1.19; favourites won more often than priced.
   - Recalibrating the market on train made it WORSE on test late in the window (log loss 0.2668
     vs 0.2596 for the raw price). The train bias was noise or a regime that ended.

B. The model adds nothing beyond the market price.
   - On its own, the model is far worse than the market price in every group:
       log loss  early 0.678 vs 0.609 | mid 0.484 vs 0.407 | late 0.419 vs 0.260
   - Given the market price, the model's weight on train is small (c = 0.17 / 0.05 / 0.01) and
     cross-validation can barely tell M1 from M0 (differences of 0.0001-0.0002).
   - On test, adding the model makes predictions worse in every group; early the whole 95% CI is
     above 0 (+0.0023 to +0.0094). The model is not just uninformative there, it hurts.
   - This is the step 5 result from the other direction: the losses come from the model disagreeing
     with a market that is better informed than it is.

C. Nothing tradeable.
   - Chosen rule (M1 early/mid, M0 late, m = 0.02): train +$20 on 1,041 trades (t = 1.39, in-sample),
     TEST -$15.26 on 226 trades, t = -2.31 -> FAIL.
   - Market-bias-only rule: train t = 2.18 in-sample, TEST -$9.60, t = -1.85. An in-sample t above 2
     that reverses out of sample, again.

What this means
  - The Binance-momentum fair-value approach has now failed four ways: live results, backtest
    reconciliation (step 4), filters (step 5), and as an information source next to the market
    (this analysis). I don't think more tuning of it is worth doing.
  - Stop paper-trading this strategy as a candidate. Keep the recorder running.
  - If research continues, it needs an information source the market may be slow to price, not
    a better transform of Binance spot. The most concrete candidate is the settlement basis: windows
    settle on Chainlink, not Binance, and the two disagreed on ~6% of window outcomes. Chainlink
    prices have been recorded since Sep 29 01:23 UTC. After a few weeks of recording, test whether
    the Chainlink-vs-Binance gap near the close predicts outcomes beyond the market price, using the
    same train/test method as here.

Caveats
  - Top-of-book 1-share fills and snapshot-only books; a finer book would not rescue B, which does
    not depend on execution.
  - B uses mid prices; it measures information content, not tradeability.
